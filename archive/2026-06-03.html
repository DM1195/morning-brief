STARTUP FUNDRAISING VENTURE CAPITAL

Anthropic Funding Pushed Startup Investment To Near-Record Levels In May As Exit Market Reopened

CRUNCHBASE NEWS · TODAY

May 2026 closed as a near-record month for startup investment — with one important asterisk: Anthropic's massive raise accounts for a disproportionate share of every headline number being celebrated right now. The exit market showed signs of thawing at the same time, with late-stage companies filing for liquidity after a prolonged drought, which is genuinely good news for the ecosystem. But the underlying distribution tells a more complicated story: seed and Series A rounds remain highly competitive to close, and the optics of a 'hot market' are already making investors more selective, not less. For non-AI founders reading these numbers as a green light, they are working from the wrong data set.

SMALL BUSINESS POLICY
New SBA policy would bar green-card holders from small business loans
THE BUSINESS JOURNALS · TODAY
The SBA is advancing a proposed rule that would make permanent residents — green-card holders — ineligible for SBA-backed loans, including the flagship 7(a) and 504 programs. This reverses decades of policy treating lawful permanent residents similarly to citizens for lending purposes. Immigrant founders represent a disproportionately high share of high-growth startups in the US, and many relied on SBA loans during early stages. Comment periods matter and the startup community needs to mobilize fast.

Plate I  ·  Oil on canvas
The Herring Net
Winslow Homer  ·  1885

Homer spent a season embedded with English fishing communities in Tynemouth and returned changed — this Maine canvas is the proof. Two fishermen haul a glistening net, their forms barely distinguishable against the grey of sea and sky, which is precisely the point: the ocean here is not backdrop but protagonist. What the work actually reveals is how realism, in Homer's hands, becomes less about accuracy than about weight — the literal weight of fish, labor, weather, and time pressing down on two anonymous men.

Plate II  ·  Oil on canvas
Peach Blossoms
Winslow Homer  ·  1878

A young woman pauses among peach blossoms at their peak, which means their peak has already begun to pass — Homer understood that spring in paint is always retrospective. Critics praised the work as distinctly American, but its flat color planes and decorative branch work reveal a deep debt to French and Japanese influences Homer would not have claimed. The tension between the nostalgia this painting performs and the cosmopolitanism it conceals is what makes it worth looking at twice.

Plate III  ·  Oil on canvas
Just Dessert
William Michael Harnett  ·  1891

One of the last three paintings Harnett completed before his death, Just Dessert arranges Maraschino liqueur, coconut, Smyrna figs, copper pitcher, and pewter tankard across a marble surface with the visible crumbs of something already eaten. The trompe l'oeil tradition demands the artist disappear, and Harnett almost does — except for those crumbs, that evidence of appetite already satisfied, which turn out to be the painting's actual subject. It's a work about aftermath masquerading as abundance.

Plate IV  ·  Oil on canvas
Herself
Robert Henri  ·  1913

Henri painted Biddy Cummings during a summer in Ireland and called the portrait Herself — the Irish endearment that means exactly what it says, refusing ornament or explanation. The thick paint and rich hues give her face a brusqueness that Henri clearly valued, though the more surprising quality is her gaze: she's looking somewhere past the artist entirely. He believed a portrait should catch "humanity in the making," which turns out to mean someone who hasn't yet decided what they're doing in a painting.
